# CHAPTER 19 — CAPITAL RECYCLING STRUCTURE

## Chapter Introduction

With scenario and foresight, political navigation, and learning and feedback structures established, we now turn to the capital longevity architecture of the institution. Capital recycling structure is the design of the systematic redeployment, compounding, and preservation of capital across cycles.

Capital recycling structure answers the fundamental questions:

- How are profits redeployed?
- What governs reinvestment vs distribution?
- How is underperforming capital extracted?
- How is capital efficiency measured?
- What is the hurdle discipline?
- How are exits reintegrated into growth?
- How is dilution managed across cycles?
- How is capital preserved during downturns?

Capital formation creates power. Capital recycling sustains it.

The institution that recycles capital efficiently will compound value over time. The institution that does not recycle capital will stagnate. Capital recycling is not optional for long-term success; it is essential.

This chapter provides the architecture for designing capital recycling structure that is disciplined, efficient, and resilient. It addresses the full spectrum of capital recycling considerations—from capital allocation governance and hurdle rates to reinvestment policy, portfolio rebalancing, divestment criteria, and reserve discipline.

## 5. Design Principles

### Principle 1: Every Deployment Must Exceed a Defined Hurdle Rate

**What does this principle mean?**

Every capital deployment must exceed a defined hurdle rate—a minimum required return.

**Why is this a principle?**

Hurdle rates ensure that capital is deployed efficiently.

**How is it implemented?**

| Implementation Method | How It Works |
|---|---|
| **Hurdle rates** | Define hurdle rates. |
| **Discipline** | Enforce hurdle discipline. |
| **Review** | Review investments against hurdles. |
| **Action** | Act on underperformers. |

**What are the trade-offs?**

| Trade-off | Consideration |
|---|---|
| **Rigidity vs. flexibility** | Hurdle rates may reduce flexibility. |
| **Discipline vs. opportunity** | Hurdle discipline may miss opportunities. |

**What happens if it is violated?**

Capital is misallocated.

### Principle 2: Capital Must Be Ranked by Marginal Return

**What does this principle mean?**

Capital must be ranked by marginal return—the return on the next unit of capital.

## 14. MICOS Scorecard

The MICOS Capital Recycling Structure Scorecard is a self-assessment tool for evaluating the maturity and durability of an institution's capital recycling structure.

### How is it used?

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1** | Complete the scorecard for each dimension. |
| **2** | Identify areas where the institution scores "No" or "Partial." |
| **3** | Prioritize remediation based on risk and impact. |
| **4** | Track progress over time. |
| **5** | Use the scorecard for due diligence and investor communication. |

### A. Allocation Discipline

| Criterion | Question | Significance | "Yes" Means |
|---|---|---|---|

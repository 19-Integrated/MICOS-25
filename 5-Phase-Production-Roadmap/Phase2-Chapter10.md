# CHAPTER 10 — OPERATIONS STRUCTURE

## Chapter Introduction

With information structure established, we now turn to the execution engine of the institution. Operations structure is the design and institutionalization of the execution engine that converts capital, assets, and strategy into measurable output.

Operations structure answers the fundamental questions:

- What exactly is produced or delivered?
- How is value created?
- What are the core workflows?
- Who executes each function?
- What controls ensure consistency?
- How are bottlenecks identified?
- How is quality enforced?
- How is scale achieved?

Ownership controls. Governance directs. Capital funds. Operations produces.

Without operational architecture, the institution remains theoretical. Strategy without execution is fantasy. Capital without operations is idle. The most beautifully designed ownership, governance, capital, legal, cashflow, risk, tax, compliance, and information structures are worthless if the institution cannot execute.

This chapter provides the architecture for designing operations structure that is efficient, scalable, and quality-controlled. It addresses the full spectrum of operational considerations—from core value chain design and workflow mapping to standard operating procedures, resource allocation, cost control, and quality assurance.

### Why Operations Structure Matters

| Dimension | Why Operations Structure Matters |
|---|---|
| **Execution** | Operations determine whether strategy is executed. |
| **Efficiency** | Operations determine the efficiency of value creation. |
| **Quality** | Operations determine the quality of output. |
| **Scalability** | Operations determine whether the institution can scale. |

| **Bankability** | Institutional capital providers evaluate operations rigorously. |

## 1. Strategic Function

### What is it?

The **Strategic Function** of the Operations Structure is to **design and institutionalize the execution engine that converts capital, assets, and strategy into measurable output.**

Operations structure is the execution engine of the institution. It determines:

- **What exactly is produced or delivered:** The outputs of the institution.
- **How is value created:** The process of value creation.
- **What are the core workflows:** The workflows that produce value.
- **Who executes each function:** The assignment of execution responsibility.
- **What controls ensure consistency:** The controls that ensure consistent output.
- **How are bottlenecks identified:** The identification of constraints.
- **How is quality enforced:** The quality assurance mechanisms.
- **How is scale achieved:** The mechanisms for scaling operations.

### Why does this matter?

Without structured operational architecture:

| Risk | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Undefined workflows** | Operations are inconsistent and inefficient. |
| **Informal or undocumented processes** | Processes are not repeatable or scalable. |
| **Founder-dependent execution** | The institution cannot function without the founder. |
| **Bottlenecks** | Operations are constrained by unidentified bottlenecks. |
| **No capacity planning** | The institution cannot meet demand. |
| **Poor cost visibility** | Costs are not understood or managed. |
| **Vendor concentration** | The institution is dependent on a single vendor. |
| **Quality control failure** | Quality is inconsistent. |
| **No contingency planning** | The institution cannot respond to disruptions. |
| **Manual processes where automation is required** | Operations are inefficient. |

### What is at stake?

| Stake | Consequence of Weak Operations Structure |
|---|---|
| **Execution** | Strategy is not executed. |
| **Efficiency** | Operations are inefficient. |
| **Quality** | Quality suffers. |
| **Cost** | Costs are too high. |
| **Scalability** | The institution cannot scale. |
| **Value** | Value is destroyed. |

### How does it connect to the framework's overall purpose?

| Framework Purpose | Operations Structure Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Bankability** | Efficient, controlled operations are essential for institutional capital. |
| **Scalability** | Operations structure must support growth. |

## 5. Design Principles

### Principle 1: Every Core Activity Must Have a Defined Workflow

**What does this principle mean?**

Every core activity must have a defined workflow—a documented sequence of steps from input to output.

**Why is this a principle?**

Undefined workflows lead to inconsistency, inefficiency, and quality issues. Defined workflows enable consistency, efficiency, and quality.

**How is it implemented?**

| Implementation Method | How It Works |
|---|---|
| **Workflow mapping** | Map all core workflows. |
| **Process documentation** | Document workflows. |
| **Standardization** | Standardize workflows. |
| **Review** | Review workflows regularly. |

**What are the trade-offs?**

| Trade-off | Consideration |
|---|---|
| **Consistency vs. flexibility** | Standardization may reduce flexibility. |
| **Documentation vs. efficiency** | Documentation requires resources. |

**What happens if it is violated?**

Operations are inconsistent, inefficient, and of poor quality.

---

**What does this principle mean?**

Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) must exist for all repeatable processes. SOPs ensure consistent execution.

**Why is this a principle?**

Repeatable processes must be executed consistently. SOPs provide the guidance for consistent execution.

**How is it implemented?**

| Implementation Method | How It Works |
|---|---|
| **SOP creation** | Create SOPs for repeatable processes. |
| **SOP training** | Train staff on SOPs. |
| **SOP review** | Review SOPs regularly. |
| **SOP updating** | Update SOPs as processes change. |

**What are the trade-offs?**

| Trade-off | Consideration |
|---|---|
| **Consistency vs. flexibility** | SOPs may reduce flexibility. |
| **Documentation vs. efficiency** | SOPs require resources. |

**What happens if it is violated?**

### Principle 3: Operational Authority Must Be Clearly Assigned

**What does this principle mean?**

Operational authority must be clearly assigned. Everyone must know who has authority over which decisions.

**Why is this a principle?**
